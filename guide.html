The basics

Voltball is a fantasy league built directly on your Zappies Reborn collection. Every held Zappy is a real player. Each week you build an 8-player roster, choose a formation and a Tempo, and your team plays out live — quarter by quarter, broadcast at a real pace, not resolved instantly behind the scenes.

Registration happens once, at the start of a season. Lineups get set — and can be changed — every week, up until that week's matches actually air.

Building your roster

You need 8 roster-eligible Zappies held in your connected wallet. Each week, you slot all 8 into four positions:

  • QB — the anchor of the lineup
  • Striker — leans primarily on VLT, with SPK as a secondary factor
  • Mid — leans primarily on SPK, with INS as a secondary factor
  • Guard — leans primarily on INS, with VLT as a secondary factor

Position matters as much as which Zappy you pick — the same Zappy can be a standout at one position and unremarkable at another, since each position weighs a different pair of stats.

Season-allocated stats

Here's something that surprises people: the VLT/INS/SPK values your Zappies play with this season aren't fixed forever. Once per season, at the moment the season is created, every Zappy in the entire collection gets a fresh, randomly-allocated performance band — Legendary, All-Pro, Starter, Backup, or Practice Squad — and its stats for that season are borrowed from a real peer currently in that band.

Rarer trait combinations get better odds, not a guarantee:

Worth knowing: a rare Zappy can never roll below Starter, and only a Tier 1 Zappy can ever hit Legendary — that's what makes rarity mean something concrete, not just "slightly better odds." But a Tier 1 Zappy can still land on a middling Starter season. Rarity buys odds, not outcomes.

Controlled plays it safer — smaller swings, a better average outcome. Aggressive swings bigger both ways — higher ceiling, higher risk. 5.5 is the default, right in the Standard range. Formation and Tempo work together, not independently: an Aggressive Tempo on a Defense-heavy formation plays very differently than the same Tempo on Offense.

How a match plays out

Voltball isn't scored like traditional football — there's no downs or possessions changing hands. It's one continuous Voltage formula, resolved quarter by quarter, driven by your roster's real stats, your formation and Tempo choices, and your coach's signature play if you have one.

Matches broadcast at a real pace once they kick off — a kickoff post goes out with a link to watch live, and the full recap (final score, quarter-by-quarter breakdown, and your coach's flavor text if their play fired) posts once playback would actually be finished. Every quarter gets real commentary, even a quiet one — nothing's ever a fake number dressed up as an event.

What actually decides the outcome

No single choice guarantees a win. Here's the real formula, not a description of its shape.

Position weighting — the actual split

Striker, Mid, and Guard each weigh two stats, not one — 70% from a primary stat, 30% from a secondary:

PositionPrimary (70%)Secondary (30%)
StrikerVLTSPK
MidSPKINS
GuardINSVLT

These weights are mean-normalized against the full 1,678-Zappy collection, so no position starts with an inherent edge — the 70/30 split is deliberate, not accidental.

QB — not a pooled weight, a multiplier

QB works differently from the other three positions entirely. It doesn't pool with anyone — your QB's own SPK stat drives a multiplier on your team's whole base offense for that quarter:

qb_multiplier = 1.0 + (QB's SPK − 54.61) × 0.0006, clamped to [0.95, 1.05]

54.61 is the real collection-wide average SPK, so a perfectly average QB is exactly neutral (1.0×, no effect at all). A strong QB (SPK in the 90s, near the real top of the collection) lifts the whole team's offense by roughly 2%; a weak one drags it down by up to 5% at the floor. Real, but deliberately modest — an earlier, more aggressive version of this let QB alone produce a 100% win rate in extreme cases, which defeated the point of it being one input among several. This version caps a common QB gap at roughly a 56% edge, not a lock.

The full quarter formula

Here's the actual calculation, in order, for one team in one quarter:

striker_component = Striker_pool(VLT) × 0.119 + Striker_pool(SPK) × 0.0326
mid_component        = Mid_pool(SPK) × 0.077 + Mid_pool(INS) × 0.0368
base_offense         = striker_component + mid_component

defense_reduction = min(opponent Guard_pool(INS) × 0.0005, 0.25)  // capped at 25% — defense can never fully shut you out
turnover_bonus     = own Guard_pool(INS) × 0.070 + own Guard_pool(VLT) × 0.0421

quarter_voltage = (base_offense × (1 − defense_reduction) + turnover_bonus)
                 × Average Multiplier  // Tempo's average-shift, see below
                 × qb_multiplier

A team's own Guard pool does double duty — it reduces how much the opponent's defense can cut into your offense (indirectly, via the opponent's own defense_reduction math) and directly adds turnover_bonus on top. That's the whole deterministic core; random quarter events (Lightning, Cold Streak, Trick Play, and the rest) and coach signatures layer on separately, after this.

Tempo — actually two separate dials, not one

This is a genuine correction to how it's often described. Tempo doesn't touch the deterministic formula above at all — it only scales the random event layer, and it does that through two independent numbers that move in opposite directions:

Controlled (1.0)Aggressive (10.0)
Average Multiplier1.06×0.94×
Volatility Multiplier (event size & odds)0.5×1.6×

The Average Multiplier is the real average tradeoff — it multiplies the whole quarter's score directly, so Controlled is genuinely worth about 6% more on average, Aggressive about 6% less. The Volatility Multiplier is the bigger effect by far — it scales how often random events fire and how big they are when they do, more than tripling in strength from one end of the dial to the other. In practice: Tempo is primarily a variance dial, with a real but comparatively modest average-outcome tradeoff layered underneath it. Cranking to full Aggressive doesn't mainly change your expected score — it mainly changes how wildly a single match can swing away from it.

Standings & playoffs

Every result updates the standings — win/loss record, tracked all season. At the end of the regular season, the top 4 teams make the playoffs: 1 vs 4 and 2 vs 3 in the semifinal, both winners meet in the championship the following week. Win it, and you're the season's champion.

The weekly rhythm

  • Lineups can be set, and changed, any time before that week's matches actually air — locking in isn't final until kickoff
  • Matches for the week go live in a staggered broadcast through the day, not all at once
  • Scores and standings stay hidden for a match until it's actually finished airing — even your own result, checked on your own team page, respects this. No spoilers, ever.
